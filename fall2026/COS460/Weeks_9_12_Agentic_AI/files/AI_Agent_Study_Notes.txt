AI AGENT FUNDAMENTALS: STUDY NOTES
1. LARGE LANGUAGE MODELS AND AI AGENTS
A large language model, or LLM, primarily generates text based on a user’s prompt and the context available to it. An AI agent goes beyond generating text. It works toward a goal, makes decisions, may use tools or knowledge sources, and can adjust its approach based on results.
A simple way to remember the difference is:
LLMs respond. AI agents act toward a goal.
2. FOUR CHARACTERISTICS OF AI AGENTS
AI agents are commonly described as:
Autonomous: They can perform some actions without continuous human direction.
Goal-oriented: Their actions are directed toward a defined objective.
Tool-using: They may interact with databases, APIs, software, search systems, or knowledge bases.
Adaptive: They may change their plan or response based on feedback, observations, or changing conditions.
3. SIMPLE AGENT WORKFLOW
A simple AI-agent workflow includes five stages:
Understand: Interpret the user’s request and identify the goal.
Plan: Determine the steps needed to address the request.
Identify resources: Determine which tools, files, data, or knowledge sources are needed.
Execute: Use the selected resources to complete the task.
Handle obstacles: Ask for clarification, correct errors, try another approach, or explain a limitation.
4. WHEN AN AI AGENT MAY BE USEFUL
An agent may be useful when a task requires:
- Multiple steps
- Dynamic decision-making
- Changing or current information
- Knowledge retrieval
- Tool use
- Adjustment based on feedback
5. WHEN AN AI AGENT MAY NOT BE NECESSARY
An agent may be unnecessary when:
- The task has a simple fixed answer
- The task can be completed using deterministic rules
- A basic prompt is sufficient
- Incorrect or inconsistent output would create unacceptable risk
6. RESPONSIBLE AI
Responsible agent design should consider:
- Fairness
- Explainability
- Privacy
- Security
- Safety
- Human control
- Accuracy
- Robustness
- Governance
- Transparency
Higher-risk actions require stronger safeguards and more human oversight.
7. HUMAN IN THE LOOP
Human-in-the-loop means that a person reviews, approves, corrects, or stops an AI system’s actions.
Human review is especially important when:
- An action cannot easily be reversed
- A decision has serious consequences
- The AI system is uncertain
- A law or policy requires human approval
8. AGENT EVALUATION
Agent evaluation may occur at three levels:
System level: Measures overall speed, reliability, efficiency, and resource use.
Task level: Measures whether the agent successfully completes its assigned goal.
Tool level: Measures whether the agent selects and uses an appropriate tool or knowledge source.
A useful evaluation should consider accuracy, task success, transparency, safety, and the need for human correction.
